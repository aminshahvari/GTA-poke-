## 2. Celestia restaurant pilot — claim-by-claim
- **"The current invite URL" — no Celestia URL exists.** Nothing named Celestia exists in the repository, the bridge, or Muse's records. A pilot URL can only be the owner's group link, generated in Director at pilot start. Muse cannot validate a specific live code without redeeming it (a production data change — not performed). At code level the format is sound: unguessable code, cap, expiry, revocation all verified (§1).
- **Registration works end-to-end — VERIFIED (staging, identical server code); production NOT TESTED by Muse.** Joiners should expect: name + PIN + agreement, immediate membership of the *one* Blindrise community, and auto-enrolment into any open tournament. The venue must be briefed that this is what "joining" means today.
- **QR scans on iPhone and Android — mechanically VERIFIED, artifact UNVERIFIED.** A camera scan opens the URL in the phone's browser; the welcome screen picks up the invite (verified). No QR artifact (image, print file) exists to inspect, so print quality cannot be verified. Acceptance spec for any print run: encode the full URL at error-correction level Q or H; minimum 25 mm printed size plus a full quiet zone; matte stock (restaurant lighting/glare); a test scan at arm's length on a mid-range Android and an iPhone before printing; and a short human-readable fallback under the code. Note the production URL is a long `onrender.com` subdomain — longer URLs make denser, less forgiving codes; a proper domain materially improves scan reliability and trust (the blindrise.com purchase remains blocked on the vault-password issue).
- **Attribution — PARTIAL.** A dedicated group link gives an exact join count (its `uses` record) and the joiners become ordinary members the Director can see. Limits: the pilot consumes the owner's **only** open group-link slot for its duration (it cannot run alongside another group campaign); production does not group joiner names by link in the Director UI (that reporting is a campaign-build feature); there is no label field, so the link's purpose lives in the owner's head and the printed material.
- **A genuine Celestia-specific membership flow — DOES NOT EXIST.** No venue page, no Celestia branding, no separate membership, no Celestia space inside the app. **A personal or group invitation URL must not be described — in print or in person — as an independently functioning Celestia club.** Honest pilot language: "Celestia invites its guests to join Blindrise, a private community club — play, connect, and meet local businesses." Anything stronger is a misrepresentation NO-GO.
- **Pilot success indicators (measurable with today's product):** scans→registrations conversion (link `uses` vs printed quantity/distribution), completed registrations (accounts created via the link), consenting active members (business profiles created + Opportunity posts by pilot joiners), repeat participation (pilot joiners entering a second tournament or returning in week 2+), attributable venue engagement (joiners naming Celestia in their profile/area field, plus the venue's own count of guest inquiries). All require the per-link count and a simple cohort list — both producible from the store by the owner today; tournament lifecycle logging (strategy item 9) would make repeat-participation measurable without database access and should be pulled earlier than days 31–60 if the pilot proceeds.

## 3. Multi-club security architecture — evaluation
Claude's gap analysis (§4 of the strategy review) is accurate; Muse verified its load-bearing claims in code (one `kv` store with global key prefixes; one `owner` record; one `tourney` state; globally unique `name:` keys; sessions with no club scope). Against the directive's eight areas: **shared identity** — PARTIAL (one identity space exists, which a network wants, but membership is global, not per-club); **club-level permissions** — ABSENT (no club-admin or business-admin role); **business administrator authorization** — ABSENT; **tenant isolation** — NOT APPLICABLE today (nothing to isolate) and therefore UNVERIFIED as a capability; **member consent & communications** — PARTIAL (push notices exist; consent copy gaps per §1); **privacy & data retention** — PARTIAL (strong contact-protection engineering; no retention/deletion tooling or policy on record); **abuse prevention & moderation** — VERIFIED for one club (reports with auto-hide at 3, spam windows, owner mute/kick/remove, household flagging); **cross-club discovery** — ABSENT (no second club, no directory of clubs). **Demanded acceptance tests before any multi-club claim:** an automated boundary suite that, for every endpoint and WebSocket message class, proves club A's members cannot read, write, watch, chat into, or infer club B's data (including invite cross-use and session-scope probes), run in CI on the exact release SHA. No such suite can exist until tenancy does. The strategy's interim model — **one deployment per club** — gives isolation by construction and is the only defensible near-term operator path; its cost is operational (per-club OWNER_CODE custody, backups, database plan/expiry management — note the current production database expires **2026-11-14**) and it provides **no shared discovery**, so it does not by itself deliver the vision's "one shared, discoverable network."

## 5. Commercial and community strategy — challenges
Claude's strategy review is unusually honest (it labels verified/code-read/inferred and parks the Gemini mapping as empty), and its stabilization-first sequencing matches Muse's gates. Challenges that stand: **(a)** the operator offering (priority 3) assumes restaurant/club operators want this — no operator interview, LOI or pilot commitment is on record; demand is UNVERIFIED, and Celestia is precisely the experiment that should test it before any operator build. **(b)** "White-label name and branding" is not a config that exists — brand strings live in the client; per-club branding is real code work plus audit per surface, so the M effort is optimistic. **(c)** One-deployment-per-club multiplies the exact operational weaknesses already open on the flagship (manual backups, an expiring database, a single shared OWNER_CODE pattern). **(d)** No funnel or retention metric is defined anywhere in the strategy; free access is treated as low-friction, not as adoption evidence — the pilot KPIs in §2 close this. **(e)** Differentiation that is VERIFIED (provable fairness, contact-privacy engineering, no-real-money design) is largely invisible to restaurant guests; the differentiation a guest can feel (belonging, a premium room, a living local network) is a brand claim — UNVERIFIED as market differentiation until a pilot produces retention numbers. **(f)** The rewards shop (§1) contradicts the ecosystem's own positioning if prizes gain prominence: benefits earned by game outcomes are the pattern regulators and the founder's own legal notes treat carefully; it needs qualified Ontario review before any promotion, and must stay entirely out of the restaurant pilot.
